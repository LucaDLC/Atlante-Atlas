HOG: histograms of gradients

Lecture 9 and the exercises of week 11 (HoG, HoG classifier). HOG (Histogram of Oriented Gradients) turns an image into a vector of features that describes the shape: the directions of the edges, cell by cell. It combines the gradients of week 8 and the classifiers of week 10. Exam questions Q6 (b) and Q8 (c).

How it is built

Check: in an image with a single vertical edge (black on the left, white on the right), the cells next to the edge put all the weight in one interval, that of the horizontal direction of the gradient.

Why it works better than the pixels

  • Light: the gradients look at differences, not absolute values, and the block normalisation removes the contrast.
  • Small shifts: inside a cell of 8 × 8 what counts is how many directions there are, not exactly where.
  • Shape: a standing person has many vertical edges (legs, torso) in similar positions: the HOG vector captures it.
  • Limits: large rotations and changes of scale change the cells; the parameters (cells, intervals) are chosen by hand. Convolutional networks learn similar filters by themselves (week 14).

Review question of week 9: why does choosing good features often matter more than the classifier? With HOG, classes that in pixel space are intertwined become almost separable by a hyperplane: a linear classifier is enough. It is the same idea as the polar coordinates for the ring.
