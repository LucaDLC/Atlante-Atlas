Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

The variance of the data in the direction of a unit vector v is vᵀCv. If v is an eigenvector of C: vᵀCv = λ vᵀv = λ. With C = [[2, 1], [1, 2]] and v = (1, 1)/√2: vᵀCv = 3 (verified). So λᵢ = σᵢ² and σᵢ = √λᵢ, as the assignment recalls.

The curse of dimensionality

With many features the space behaves in a counter-intuitive way: it is the curse of dimensionality, one of the goals of week 12. Three effects, all measurable with a few lines of NumPy.

1 · The data become sparse. To cover an interval with 10 points, 10 are enough; a square with the same density needs 100, a cube 1000, a space of 10 dimensions 10¹⁰. With a fixed number of examples, more features means points ever more isolated.

2 · The volume escapes to the corners. The sphere inscribed in the cube [−1, 1]ᵈ occupies 78.5% of the cube in 2D, 52% in 3D and only 0.25% in 10D. Almost all the points are far from the centre, near the corners.

3 · All distances look alike. Taking 500 random points in the cube [0, 1]ᵈ, the ratio between (maximum distance − minimum distance) and minimum distance from a point is about 29 in 2D, 2.5 in 10D, 0.41 in 100D and 0.11 in 1000D. The "nearest neighbour" is not much nearer than the others: methods based on distances, such as k-NN, lose their meaning.

The remedy is that of this week: reduce the dimensions before measuring distances, with PCA (the first components keep almost all the variance, like the 5 components out of 146 of the faces) or by choosing a few good features.
