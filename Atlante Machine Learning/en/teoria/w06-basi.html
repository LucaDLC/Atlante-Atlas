Vector spaces, independence and bases

Week 6 (Grasple exercises) and lecture 6 ("change of basis"). A vector can be described with different coordinates according to the basis chosen. It is the key of PCA (week 12) and explains when least squares has a single solution. DW chapters 9 and 10; exam question Q4 (d).

Span: everything that can be reached

A vector space is a set where sums and multiples of vectors remain in the set (the 8 rules of the book, not to be learned by heart). A subspace is a piece that respects the same rule: for example a line or a plane that pass through the origin. A line that does not pass through 0 is not a subspace.

Linear independence

v₁, …, vₙ are linearly independent if the only way to obtain 0 is with all weights at 0: c₁v₁ + … + cₙvₙ = 0 ⇒ c₁ = … = cₙ = 0. Put in words: none can be written as a combination of the others.

In ℝⁿ there are at most n independent vectors. A matrix with more columns than rows necessarily has dependent columns. In models: more parameters than training points ⇒ infinitely many solutions (the polynomial of degree 4 with 3 points, polynomials).

Basis and dimension

A basis of a space is a set of vectors that (1) are independent and (2) generate the whole space (span = space). Then every vector is written in only one way as their combination: the weights are its coordinates in that basis. The number of vectors of a basis is the dimension: 2 in ℝ², 3 in ℝ³.

The standard basis of ℝ² is e1 = (1, 0), e2 = (0, 1): put as columns they form the identity matrix I. But any pair of independent vectors is a basis of the plane.

Change of basis

If the vectors of the basis have length 1 and are perpendicular to each other (orthonormal), the matrix Q has QᵀQ = I, so Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ: each coordinate is simply an inner product with a vector of the basis. PCA chooses precisely an orthonormal basis suited to the data (week 12).
