Clustering: k-means, mean shift, agglomerative

Lecture 12 and the exercise of week 13 (Clustering, on the sequences of poses). Unsupervised learning: no labels, one looks for groups of similar data. Three methods with three different ideas of "group". Exam question Q11. DW chapter 23.

K-means

Elbow method, on 300 points generated with 3 groups (make_blobs, KMeans): the inertia always falls as k increases (with k = N it is 0), but up to 3 it falls a lot and soon after little. The "elbow" suggests k = 3. Pros: simple and fast. Limits: k must be chosen; round groups of similar size; sensitive to the scale of the features and to outliers.

With Anaconda on Windows KMeans prints "KMeans is known to have a memory leak on Windows with MKL…". It is not an error: the results are right. To make it disappear: the environment variable OMP_NUM_THREADS=2 before importing sklearn.

The same 300 points, MeanShift(bandwidth=h). h too small: every small crowding becomes a cluster. h too large: everything merges. h has the same role as k, put in another way (Task 11).

Agglomerative clustering

On the same data with 3 clusters: ward gives groups of 105, 100 and 95 points; single linkage 199, 1 and 100: an isolated point becomes a cluster on its own and two nearby groups "chain" together. The linkage changes the result.

In the exercise 1403 sequences of poses are grouped: the centre of a k-means cluster is an average pose, to be interpreted by looking at it. Without labels there is no accuracy: one evaluates with the inertia, the stability, and above all with the sense of the groups found.

(a) k-means and mean shift, (b) k-means and agglomerative, (c) k-means and elbow. To bring: the steps of k-means with why it converges and the local minima, the inertia and the elbow, mean shift as climbing towards the density with h, the hierarchy and the linkages, pros and limits compared.
