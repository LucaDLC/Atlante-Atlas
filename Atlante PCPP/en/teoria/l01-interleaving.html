Interleaving and race condition

With two turnstiles, CounterThreads2.java almost always prints fewer than 20 000. The reason: counter++ looks like a single operation but it is two steps, and between the two another thread can slip in. Here you build yourself the interleavings that make increments disappear.

counter++ is not atomic

The turnstile problem with two threads comes from here. An operation that looks single, like adding 1 to a counter, is for the machine a sequence of smaller steps. If two threads run their sequences mixed together, the result can be wrong. To understand which mixtures are possible, each operation is broken into its steps and the steps are numbered.

An instruction is atomic if it is executed as a single, indivisible operation. Being written on one line does not make it atomic.

Build the lost update

Two threads run the body of the loop once. The right result is counter = 2. Try to get 1, then open "All possible interleavings".

<thread>(<step>), <thread>(<step>), … — for example t1(1), t1(2), t2(1), t2(2). It is a sequence of operations of the concurrent program, not of a single thread. Each thread must respect its own order: t1(2) cannot come before t1(1).

Race condition and data race

They are the two most used words of the course, and the most confused. One concerns the result of the program, the other the accesses to memory. They often happen together (as with counter++), but they are independent: one can have one without the other.

Race condition

The result depends on the interleaving. It is a property of the output.

Data race (definition of lecture 1)

Two threads access the same memory location and at least one writes. It is a property of the accesses. In lecture 3 the definition becomes more precise.

Why with 100 loops it almost always gives 200 (exercise 1.1.2)

With few loops one thread often finishes all its work before the other starts, or within a single turn of the scheduler: the "bad" interleavings, in which one thread slips in between the read and the write of the other, become rare. Rare does not mean impossible: 200 is not guaranteed, because the scheduler can always suspend a thread between (1) and (2).

The minimum value (exercise 1.1.8)

Without locks and with counts = 3, what is the smallest value the program can print? Wrong intuition: 3. Verified by enumerating all the interleavings: the minimum is 2, and it stays 2 even with 20 million loops.

"The minimum is 2. t1 reads 0 and is suspended; t2 completes all but its last iteration; t1 writes 1, overwriting t2's work; t2 reads 1 and is suspended; t1 completes its remaining iterations; finally t2 writes 2, overwriting all of t1's increments. The same interleaving works for any number of iterations greater than 1."
