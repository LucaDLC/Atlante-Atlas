Exam

How the oral exam works

A 30-minute oral, with no preparation time, between 21 and 29 January 2027. Questions on any part of the programme. Approved assignments are required to be admitted. Below is everything the official documents say.

The official description says "80% of the hand-ins must be approved", with a final hand-in to recover those not approved; LearnIT and the slides of lecture 1 say "at least 5 assignments" out of 6. They are the same threshold (5/6 ≈ 83%). Slide 6 talks about 13 sets of exercises, LearnIT about 12: the number of assignments counts, and it is 6 in both.

What to expect

  • No preparation: the question arrives and you answer at once. Train yourself to reason out loud, in English, on small examples (how to answer).
  • The slides of lecture 1: "Oral feedback sessions aim at preparing you for the exam". The feedback sessions on the assignments are the dress rehearsal: same type of questions, same vocabulary.
  • The course stresses reasoning about the correctness of concurrent programs: interleaving, happens-before, thread-safety, linearizability. From the description: "the course places emphasis on how to reason about the correctness of concurrent programs".
  • The challenging exercises "are meant for students aiming at high grades in the exam": they are not needed to pass the assignments, but they are the level of the high grades.
  • Past exams: being an oral, none are published. The training material is the questions in the blue boxes of the slides and the exercises: training questions.

The Examination Syllabus (the compulsory reading for the exam) comes out on LearnIT by the last lecture. Lecture 14 (30 November) is dedicated to preparing for the exam. When they come out, this page must be updated.
