Trap pairs

Two problems with the same input and one different word: how one detail changes the paradigm, with the real pairs from the exams.

The principle

The teacher builds the exams in pairs: same input, one different word. Learn to see the word, not the topic. When two problems in the same exam look alike, one is almost always the easy version (greedy or traversal) and the other the hard one (DP or NP-hard). Ask yourself: does this word break the exchange argument? does it remove the order? does it force choosing a subset of nodes?

Practical rule

When two problems in the same exam look alike, the word that separates them is the detail that counts. Do not stop at the topic: re-read the verb and the constraint.

Mistakes that cost points

  • «Sort the input» without saying for what and in which direction. The text forbids it explicitly.
  • Time without parameters: writing O(n) when the problem has N and K. Declare what the symbols mean.
  • Reinventing: describing the BFS line by line instead of citing it. It is enough to say "BFS" and its cost.
  • Reduction the wrong way round: starting from the new problem as if it were given. Start from the known problem, as in the table of Easy look-alikes. In question (d) it gets 0 points.
  • OPT without meaning: a recurrence without saying what OPT(i, j) represents and the range of the indices.
  • Counterexample with ties: if the algorithm could choose well by breaking a tie, the counterexample is not «guaranteed to fail». The counterexample must hold for every choice.
