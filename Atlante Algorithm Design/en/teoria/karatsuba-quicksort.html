Karatsuba and quicksort

Two divide and conquer algorithms that beat the naive method: Karatsuba multiplies large numbers with three multiplications instead of four, quicksort sorts with partitions around a pivot.

Karatsuba: three multiplications instead of four

Multiplying two numbers of n digits at school costs n² (four multiplications of half size). Karatsuba splits each number into two halves: x = a·10^k + b, y = c·10^k + d. It computes ac, bd and (a+b)(c+d): the mixed term ad + bc comes out by difference, without one more multiplication.

Recurrence T(n) = 3T(n/2) + O(n): O(n^1.585).

Quicksort: partition around the pivot

Choose a pivot. Move the smaller elements to the left, the larger (or equal) ones to the right. The pivot is in its final place. Then sort the two parts recursively.

With a random pivot the expected cost is O(n log n). With a pivot that is always the worst (the minimum or the maximum) it becomes O(n²): that is why the pivot is chosen at random.

At the exam

Multiplication of large integers → Karatsuba, O(n^log₂3). Sorting on average → randomised quicksort, O(n log n) expected. Phrase: "Karatsuba uses three recursive multiplications of half size, so T(n) = 3T(n/2) + O(n) = O(n^1.585)."
