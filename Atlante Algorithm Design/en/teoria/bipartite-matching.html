Bipartite matching via flow

Matching the maximum number of elements of one set with elements of another, only along allowed pairs. It is transformed into a flow: each unit of flow is a pair.

Transforming into a flow

Given two sets L and R and the allowed pairs between them: add a source s with arcs of capacity 1 towards every node of L, orient the pairs from L towards R with capacity 1, and add a sink t with arcs of capacity 1 from every node of R.

The max flow is the maximum matching. The capacities 1 on s → L and R → t prevent using a node twice.

The cost and the variants

With Ford-Fulkerson: O(mn). With Hopcroft-Karp: O(m√n). If the limits are not 1 (an element can be used twice), only the capacity of the arc changes.

Kőnig: in a bipartite graph the minimum vertex cover equals the maximum matching. Hall: a perfect matching exists if, for every subset of L, its neighbourhood has at least as many elements.

At the exam

Assigning each element of one type to one of the other, with limits → flow. Phrase: "Let s be a source with arcs of capacity 1 to every node of L, and t a sink with arcs of capacity 1 from every node of R. Max flow equals the maximum matching; by integrality the arcs with flow 1 are the pairs. Time O(mn)."
