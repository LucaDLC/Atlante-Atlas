Minimum spanning tree: Kruskal and Prim

Connecting all the nodes of a graph at the minimum total cost: the minimum spanning tree (MST) problem. Two greedy algorithms solve it: Kruskal, which adds the cheapest edges, and Prim, which grows a single tree.

The problem

A spanning tree of a connected graph is a subset of edges that connects all the nodes without cycles. It always has n − 1 edges. The minimum spanning tree (MST) is the one of minimum total cost: minimum sum of the weights.

The cost does not depend on the root: a tree has no intrinsic root. The question is only which edges to keep.

Kruskal: take the cheapest edge

Rule: sort the edges by increasing weight. For each edge u–v: if u and v are in different components, take it and merge the components; otherwise discard it, because it would close a cycle. Stop at n − 1 edges.

To know whether u and v are already connected one uses the union-find (dedicated page).

Why it works: the cut property

The cut property: given a cut of the graph (a division of the nodes into two groups), the cheapest edge crossing the cut belongs to some MST. Kruskal always takes this kind of edge: the cheapest one that connects two components.

Symmetric, the cycle property: the most expensive edge of a cycle is in no MST. That is why you discard it when it closes a cycle.

Prim: grow a single tree

Prim starts from a node and grows a single tree: at each step it adds the cheapest edge leaving the tree towards an external node. It is the same scheme as Dijkstra: a priority queue on the external nodes, with the minimum distance from the tree.

Cost O(m log n) with a binary heap, like Kruskal. Prim is more convenient when the graph is dense; Kruskal when the edges are already sorted or when you need the global order.

At the exam

Connecting everything at minimum cost → MST. Write: "Sort the edges by non-decreasing weight. Add an edge if its endpoints are in different components (union-find). The result is a minimum spanning tree by the cut property. Time O(m log n)."

If the question asks to prove the correctness, cite the cut property and do the exchange argument on the discarded edge.
