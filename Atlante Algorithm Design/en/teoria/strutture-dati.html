Data structures: which to choose and what it costs

At the exam the data structures serve to justify the time. Choose the structure by looking at the operation you repeat most often, and multiply its cost by how many times you repeat it.

Amortised cost

An amortised cost is a bound on the sum of the costs of a sequence of operations, divided by the number of operations. A single operation can cost a lot, but the sequence does not. Example: the binary counter. Each increment changes many bits, but in 8 increments the total changes are 15: about 2 per operation, therefore O(1) amortised.

Dijkstra and the queue

Dijkstra with binary heap: O(m log n). With an unsorted array: O(n²), good for dense graphs. With a Fibonacci heap: O(m + n log n), more theoretical. The exam accepts the binary heap as the standard answer.

At the exam

Write the structure and its cost per operation, then multiply. Phrase: "We store the graph as adjacency lists; scanning the neighbours of v costs O(deg(v)), so the total over all vertices is O(m)."
