Closest pair of points

Given n points in the plane, find the pair with minimum distance. Divide and conquer solves it in O(n log n) instead of O(n²).

The idea: divide with a line

Sort the points by x and divide them with a vertical line into two equal halves. The closest pair is on the left, on the right, or crosses the line. The first two are solved recursively: let δ be the minimum distance found.

The strip

For the pair that crosses the line it is enough to look at a strip of width 2δ around the line: a point outside the strip is already further than δ from any point of the other half. Inside the strip, sorted by y, each point must be compared only with a few neighbours (at most a handful): the work is linear.

The cost

Recurrence: T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n), so O(n log n). The sort by x is done only once, at the start.

At the exam

Closest pair → divide and conquer with strip: O(n log n). Phrase: "Sort by x; split with a vertical line; recurse on both halves getting δ; check the strip of width 2δ, sorted by y. T(n) = 2T(n/2) + O(n)."
