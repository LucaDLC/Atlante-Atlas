Brute force and branching

When a problem is NP-hard, the only guaranteed way to the exact solution is to try the candidates. Brute force enumerates them all, branching branches only where needed. The cost is exponential, but it often depends on the size of the solution, not on n.

Enumerating with bits

A subset of n elements is represented by a number k from 0 to 2ⁿ − 1: bit i of k says whether element i is inside. In Java: k & (1 << i) is non-zero if bit i is on. For each subset you check the condition and keep the best.

Branching: in or out

Branching does the same thing as recursion: for an element, two branches, in or out. With pruning, the branches that cannot improve are discarded.

Example: vertex cover with small k. Take an edge u–v: at least one of the two is in the cover. Branch on G − u with k − 1 and on G − v with k − 1. Depth k, 2ᵏ branches: exponential in k, not in n.

When it is useful and why it is correct

Every problem in NP has a certificate verifiable in polynomial time. So trying all the certificates is always correct, only slow. To understand the costs: O(2ⁿ · poly(n)) for the subsets, O(n! · poly(n)) for the permutations, O(2ᵏ · kn) for the branching on vertex cover.

Permutations in lexicographic order

A permutation of 1, 2, 3 is a way of writing the three numbers in a row: 123, 132, 213, 231, 312, 321. There are 3! = 6. The lexicographic order is the dictionary one: you compare the first number, then the second, and so on.

To go from the permutation p to the next one, there are three moves:

  1. From the right I look for the first point where the row stops increasing: the position i with p[i] < p[i+1]. The suffix to the right of i is decreasing: you cannot increase it by staying inside.
  2. The number at i (the pivot) must be increased: I look from the right for the smallest number greater than p[i], and I swap them.
  3. The suffix to the right of i is decreasing: I reverse it, so it becomes as small as possible (increasing).

Generating all n! permutations costs O(n!) in total: on average each step touches a very short suffix. With n = 13 there are more than 6 billion: brute force only works for small n.

At the exam

NP-hard problem with a small instance, or with a small parameter (k) → brute force or branching. Phrase: "Enumerate all subsets using bit masks; each subset is checked in polynomial time. Time O(2ⁿ · poly(n))."
