All the operations on LazyList

Every operation of week 4, with what it does in a line and the real result on l = LazyList(1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Next to it is who wrote it: the teacher in the file, or you in an exercise.

Who wrote what

Already in the file (teacher)

In the enum LazyList: Empty, Cons, headOption, tail, foldRight, foldLeft, find.

In the companion object LazyList: empty, cons, apply (it is what you use when writing LazyList(1, 2, 3)).

Written by you

toList, take, drop, takeWhile, forAll, exists, takeWhile1, headOption1, map, filter, append, flatMap, from, to, naturals, fibs, unfold, fibsUnfold, mapUnfold, takeUnfold, takeWhileUnfold, zipWith.

At the top of the file: "we are using our own lazy lists, not the standard library". None, Some, Option, List, Nil come from the standard library; the LazyList, however, is the course's own. The standard library has its own scala.collection.immutable.LazyList (with #:: instead of cons, and also from and unfold), but it is not used in the exercises.

On LazyList(1, 2, 3, 4) with "is even": takeWhile gives List() (it stops at once on the 1), filter gives List(2, 4) (it skips and continues). In the foldRight version the difference is one word: else empty against else acc.

Look at what f returns. A value (A => B) → map. A list (A => LazyList[B]) → flatMap. With map and an f that gives lists you would get a list of lists.

The …Unfold and …1 versions

They give the same results as the originals, written in another way: takeWhile1 and headOption1 with foldRight (ex 6–7), mapUnfold, takeUnfold, takeWhileUnfold, fibsUnfold with unfold (ex 12–13). The code is in the pages Lazy foldRight and Infinite lists and unfold.
