What the test is like

The precise format is explained in the lecture of week 13 ("Let's talk about the exam", 24 November) and in the Examination Syllabus published on LearnIT by the last lecture. What is already known from the course documents:

  • Small programming exercises, similar to those of the homework. The course description: "most time is spent on small practical exercises resembling exam questions".
  • Open questions in English. The week 4 sheet (exercise 9): "The exam may contain open questions to be answered in English".
  • The same style rules as the exercises: no var, effects, exceptions, return, unless asked. See Rules and prohibitions.
  • Old exams: the week 1 slides say "old exams will be published". Current status in Old exams.

What you must be able to do (official objectives)

  • Design, test and run functional programs in Scala.
  • Use expressive types (polymorphism, type-level functions, higher-order types) to describe library interfaces.
  • Recognise monadic structures, use monadic libraries and design them.
  • Reason about eager and lazy evaluation, with advantages and disadvantages.
  • Reason about APIs in pure and stateful style, comparing the designs.
  • Use eager and lazy to design data structures and exploit existing lazy ones (streams).
  • Implement solutions based on research papers in library and language design.

The re-exam

It depends on how many people sign up:

21 or more sign-ups

Four-hour written exam in person (A22). Internet: full access. Printed and handwritten books and notes allowed, any e-book and software, but no AI: no language model, no GitHub Copilot.

20 or fewer sign-ups

Oral with preparation: 30 minutes of supervised preparation, then 20 minutes of oral.

The materials allowed are written this way only for the re-exam. For the ordinary exam the description says "with restrictions" without details: the Examination Syllabus will confirm them.
